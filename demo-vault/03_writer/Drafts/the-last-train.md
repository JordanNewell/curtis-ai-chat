# The Last Train

## Draft 2

The 11:47 to Bayshore was never on time. Sora had been riding it for six years and could not remember a single night when the doors hissed shut at the posted hour. The schedule, she had come to believe, was a piece of fiction the transit authority maintained out of habit — like an aging aunt who still set a place at the table for a husband twenty years gone.

She liked it that way. The lateness was a kind of permission. If the train was late, she was not waiting for it; she was just standing. There was a difference.

Tonight the platform was empty except for a man in a grey overcoat, reading a paperback by the yellow light of the schedule board. The book was small, well-thumbed, no cover she could see. He did not look up when she walked past, but she felt him notice her — the way the air changes when another person enters a room you'd thought was yours alone.

She sat on the bench at the far end. He stayed near the stairs. The right amount of distance, she thought. Not so far as to be suspicious, not so close as to be rude. New Yorkers were good at this calculus. Tourists never were.

"Excuse me."

She looked up. He was closer than she'd calculated — three feet, maybe. Holding out the book.

"Do you know this one? My daughter recommended it. I'm trying to— " He paused, searching for the word. "— to understand her better, I suppose."

The cover was a faded line drawing. A house on a hill. She did not recognize it.

"I'm sorry," she said. "I don't."

"She's fourteen." He turned the book over, as if the back might explain something the front did not. "Fourteen and reading things I can't follow. I thought— " He stopped again. "I thought if I read what she reads."

The 11:47 — which was, by the schedule board's account, now the 11:53 — rumbled somewhere in the tunnel. The lights flickered.

"That's a good impulse," Sora said.

"It doesn't work," he said. "I'm forty pages in and I don't understand any of it."

Sora almost laughed. The book could have been anything. The impulse was the thing.

"What's it about?" she asked.

He looked at the back again. The yellow light caught his face. He was younger than she'd first thought — mid-forties, tired, the kind of tired that is not from one night.

"A girl," he said. "A girl who leaves."

Sora's mother had left when Sora was nine. Not in the dramatic way — no note, no slammed door, no suspicious silence in the morning. Just a slow disappearance, like a tide going out so gradually you didn't notice the sand until you looked up and the water was gone.

She had spent twenty years writing about that kind of leaving. Three novels, two collections of stories, a memoir she'd never publish. All about women who left, and the people they left behind, and the strange geometry of a house that still had their coat in the closet.

The man was still holding out the book.

"May I?" she said.

He looked surprised. Then pleased.

The train arrived. They boarded separately — he went to the front, she took her usual seat in the third car. The book sat in her lap, the line-drawn house on its cover staring up at her.

She opened it to page forty.

## Notes for revision

- The opening needs tightening. The "schedule as fiction" metaphor is doing too much work — pick one comparison, not two.
- The man's dialogue is too polished. He's nervous; his sentences should fragment more.
- "The strange geometry of a house that still had their coat in the closet" — keep this. Don't let me cut it in the next pass.
- Page 40: what book is it? Decide by draft 3. Currently leaning *A House for Mr. Biswas* but that doesn't quite fit.
- Ending TBD. Either the train ride, or jump cut to a year later. Try both, pick in revision.
- Target length 4000 words. Currently 2847. Need one more beat — maybe the actual reading-on-the-train sequence, or Sora at home later that night.

## Pitches for next round of revision

1. **Trim the opening.** Cut "like an aging aunt..." paragraph entirely. Start with Sora, not the train's schedule.
2. **Fictional book reveal.** Decide what the book is and let Sora recognize it on page 40.
3. **The man gets off at Bayshore too.** Currently they're both heading to Bayshore but I never pay it off. Either they share the walk from the station (more) or she watches him disappear into a car (less).
4. **Sora's mother gets one specific detail.** Not a list — one. The way she held a coffee cup, maybe. Something concrete that pays off later.
