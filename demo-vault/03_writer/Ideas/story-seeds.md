# Story Seeds

Things to develop. Some have legs, some are one-liners. Circle back quarterly.

## Fiction

### The cartographer

A woman whose job is to update maps after natural disasters. She redraws coastlines, marks where roads used to be, deletes towns. The story is about the day she has to redraw her own neighborhood.

- Hook: the moment she realizes the "before" map is wrong too.
- Setting: somewhere small and specific. Maybe the Florida panhandle.

### The recommender

A music critic takes a job at a streaming company, training the recommendation algorithm. Slowly realizes her taste is changing to match what the model predicts she'll like. Is she still herself?

- Inspiration: that thing where Spotify knows you better than you do.
- Risk: too on-the-nose. Needs a specific character to ground it.

### Two sisters, one house

Sisters inherit their mother's house. One wants to sell, one wants to keep. The house is a character — water stains, broken shutters, a willow in the back. They have one weekend to decide.

- Tried this before, didn't work. What's missing: a third thing. A neighbor. A secret. Something that breaks the binary.
- Try again with: the neighbor is the mother's old lover. Both sisters knew, neither knew the other knew.

### The language model

A writer befriends a language model. Not in a sci-fi way — just uses it constantly for work. Starts noticing the model's "voice" leaking into her prose. The story is the moment she sends a draft to her editor and the editor says "this doesn't sound like you."

- Personal angle: I use AI tools for outlining. Has my prose changed?
- Could be a short story or a personal essay. Try as essay first.

## Non-fiction

### On quitting Twitter (again)

Personal essay. I've quit Twitter five times. Each time I've come back. Why? What does the cycle say about how I work?

- Vector might take this — fits their "tech & culture" frame.
- Risk: too done. Everyone's written this essay. Find the specific angle.

### The office I never went to

My company went fully remote in 2020. We had a lease on a 4000 sq ft office in Manhattan that we never occupied. We paid for it for three years. This is an essay about that empty room.

- Strong central image. Could carry 2000 words.
- May be too slight for Vector. Try HarperCollins blog first.

### How I learned to write headlines

Personal essay about my first job at a content farm in 2018. We wrote 30 headlines a day. The skill has never left me. Sometimes I catch myself writing headlines for my own life.

- Voice-forward piece. Could be 1200 words.

### The email I never sent

A piece about an email I drafted to my father for nine years and never sent. He died in 2024. The essay is the email, finally sent, too late.

- Hard to write. Worth trying.
- Not for Vector — too personal. Try Granta or Georgia Review.
